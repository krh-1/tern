# Draft v1 — candidate questions (62)

Format: Title · Scene · Question · Answers · Follow-up (if any) · Reveals

## Theme 1 — Honesty & what we owe the truth

### N01 The Invisible Year
Scene: For one year, nothing you do can ever be traced back to you. No cameras, no witnesses, no consequences.
Question: Would you live any differently?
- A: No. I'd live exactly the same.
- B: A little. Small things I normally wouldn't risk.
- C: Yes, quite a lot.
Follow-up (all): Now everyone gets the same year. Would the world be better or worse by the end?
- A: Better. Most people are decent when no one's watching.
- B: Worse. Most people are decent because someone is watching.
- C: About the same.
Reveals: whether your goodness is internal or held up by being seen, and whether you think others are like you.

### N02 The Deathbed Question
Scene: Your grandfather is dying and has hours left. He asks if the family business he spent his life building is doing well. It went bankrupt last year.
Question: What do you tell him?
- A: The truth, as gently as I can.
- B: That it's doing well.
- C: I change the subject so I don't have to lie.
Follow-up (if B or C): Years ago he told you, "Whatever happens, never lie to me. Not even at the end." Does that change your answer?
- A: Yes. I tell him the truth.
- B: No. He can't have meant this.
Reveals: comfort vs respect for someone's right to know; whose wishes count at the end.

### N03 The Reference Call
Scene: A former employee of yours was pleasant but unreliable — missed deadlines, sometimes didn't show. They've been out of work for 8 months and support two kids. A hiring manager calls you for a reference.
Question: What do you say?
- A: The honest picture, problems included.
- B: The good parts. I leave out the rest.
- C: I say I don't give references.
Follow-up (if B): The job is overnight care for elderly patients, where missing a shift means someone goes without help. Does that change what you say?
- A: Yes. I tell them about the reliability issue.
- B: No. They deserve a chance.
Reveals: whether honesty serves the person in front of you or the strangers downstream.

### N04 The Company Line
Scene: Your manager tells you to explain a delayed order to customers as a "supplier issue." The real reason is your company chose to prioritize bigger clients.
Question: What do you tell customers?
- A: The supplier line. It's my job, not my lie.
- B: Something vague but true, like "we're behind on orders."
- C: The real reason, even if it gets me in trouble.
Reveals: whether a role changes what you're personally responsible for.

### N05 The Burned Manuscript
Scene: Your best friend, before dying, made you promise to delete all of their unpublished writing without reading it. You opened one file by accident. It's extraordinary — maybe the best thing they ever wrote.
Question: Do you keep the promise?
- A: Yes. I delete everything.
- B: No. I share it with the world.
- C: I keep it private but don't delete it.
Reveals: whether promises to the dead still bind; the person's wishes vs the value of what they made.

## Theme 2 — Family, loyalty, and who comes first

### N06 The 2 A.M. Call
Scene: Your sibling calls at 2 a.m. They hit a cyclist with their car and drove away in panic. The cyclist is in the hospital and will recover. The police have no leads. Your sibling asks you to say they were with you tonight.
Question: What do you do?
- A: I cover for them.
- B: I refuse to lie, but I don't turn them in.
- C: I tell them to turn themselves in, or I will.
Follow-up (if A or B): A week later, police arrest a different person for it. That person is innocent.
- A: I tell the police the truth.
- B: I push my sibling to come forward, but I won't do it myself.
- C: I stay quiet. The case against them will probably fall apart.
Reveals: how far loyalty to family goes, and where it breaks when a stranger pays the price.

### N07 The Promise About the Home
Scene: Your parent has advanced dementia. Caring for them at home would take most of your free time for years and strain your own family. A good care facility is available. Years ago, they made you promise you'd never put them in a home.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Keep the promise and care for them at home.
- B: Move them to the facility.
- C: Keep them home, but hire help I can barely afford.
Follow-up (all): They no longer recognize you. They seem calm with anyone who is kind to them. Does that change things?
- A: Yes. The person I promised isn't really there anymore.
- B: No. It's still them, and a promise is a promise.
Reveals: whether promises bind to the person who asked or the person now in front of you; duty to parents vs to your own life.

### N08 The Draft
Scene: Your country goes to war. You believe this war is wrong. You're called up to serve.
Question: What do you do?
- A: I serve. My country asked.
- B: I refuse and accept the consequences, even prison.
- C: I serve in a role where I won't have to fight.
- D: I leave the country.
Follow-up (all): Now the war is clearly defensive — your country was invaded. Does that change your answer?
- A: Yes. I'd fight.
- B: No. I still won't fight.
Reveals: conscience vs civic duty; whether you're a pacifist or a just-war thinker.

### N09 The Cousin or the Stranger
Scene: You have $5,000 saved. Your cousin needs help buying a used car to get to a new job. The same money, given to a proven charity, would very likely save a child's life overseas.
Question: Where does the money go?
- A: My cousin.
- B: The charity.
- C: Split it.
Follow-up (if A): Your cousin could get a loan instead. It would just be harder for them.
- A: Still my cousin.
- B: Then the charity.
Reveals: how much closeness should count against a life.

### N10 The Dog or the Stranger
Scene: Your dog and a stranger are both drowning. You can only save one.
Question: Who do you save?
- A: The stranger.
- B: My dog.
Reveals: whether moral worth tracks species or bond.

## Theme 3 — Luck, desert, and fairness

### N11 Two Drivers
Scene: Two friends each have three drinks and drive home the same way, equally carefully. A child runs into the road in front of one of them. That driver hits the child. The other gets home without incident.
Question: Did they do something equally wrong?
- A: Yes. Same choice, same wrong. The rest was luck.
- B: No. The one who hit the child did something worse.
Follow-up (if A): Should they get the same punishment?
- A: Yes.
- B: No. Outcomes have to matter in the law.
Reveals: whether you judge people by what they chose or what happened.

### N12 Two Worlds
Scene: You'll be born into one of two worlds, into a random life. You don't know who you'll be. In World A, everyone has a decent, secure life, but nobody is rich. In World B, most people live much better than in World A, but 1 in 10 people are poor.
Question: Which world do you choose?
- A: World A.
- B: World B.
Follow-up (all): Now you know you'll be born into the top half. Does that change your pick?
- A: Yes, B.
- B: No — same answer.
Reveals: how you weigh security for everyone against a better average; whether self-interest shifts it.

### N13 The Proud Thing
Scene: Think of the thing in your life you're most proud of.
Question: How much of it was up to you?
- A: Mostly me. I worked for it.
- B: About half me, half luck and help.
- C: Mostly luck — timing, family, people who helped.
Follow-up (all): Does that mean you deserve it less?
- A: No. I still earned it.
- B: A little.
- C: Deserve doesn't really come into it.
Reveals: your belief in desert and self-made success, applied to yourself.

### N14 The Lifeboat
Scene: A lifeboat holds 8 but has 9 people on it. If no one leaves, it will sink and everyone dies. You're one of the 9.
Question: How should it be decided?
- A: Draw lots. Everyone takes the same chance.
- B: Ask for a volunteer.
- C: The person least likely to survive anyway.
- D: I go.
Reveals: fair procedure vs best outcome vs self-sacrifice.

## Theme 4 — Harm, sacrifice, and how far you'd go

### N15 The Pond and the Postcard
Scene: You're walking past a pond and see a small child drowning. Wading in will ruin your $800 phone. You'd go in without thinking. Now: $800 given to a proven charity would very likely save a child's life far away.
Question: Is there a real difference between the two?
- A: Yes. Being right there makes it my duty.
- B: No — and honestly, I don't live up to that.
- C: No — and I do give.
- D: The far-away child isn't my responsibility.
Reveals: the moral weight of distance, and how you handle the gap between your beliefs and your life.

### N16 The Kidney
Scene: You learn you're a match for a stranger who will die without a kidney. Surgery is safe but painful, with a small lifelong risk.
Question: Do you donate?
- A: Yes.
- B: Only for someone I know.
- C: No.
Follow-up (if A): The stranger served 20 years for murder and was released last year.
- A: Still yes.
- B: No.
Reveals: how much you'd give a stranger, and whether desert limits rescue.

### N17 The Monkeys
Scene: A medical study would cause serious suffering to 100 monkeys. It would likely lead to a treatment that saves thousands of human lives.
Question: Should the study go ahead?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
- C: Only if there's truly no other way.
Follow-up (if A or C): What if it were 100 dogs?
- A: Same answer.
- B: That feels different.
Reveals: the edges of your moral circle and whether your lines are consistent.

### N18 The Slaughterhouse Test
Scene: Imagine that to eat meat, you had to kill the animal yourself.
Question: Would you still eat meat?
- A: Yes.
- B: Less of it.
- C: No, I'd stop.
- D: I already don't.
Follow-up (if B or C): Is it wrong to pay someone else to do what you wouldn't?
- A: Yes, a little.
- B: No. That's just how work divides.
Reveals: whether your moral lines survive being close to the act.

### N19 The Weapons Job
Scene: You're offered a well-paid job designing weapons systems. You're against the project. If you say no, it goes to someone who'd do it more carelessly — with more civilian harm.
Question: Do you take the job?
- A: Yes. Better me than someone worse.
- B: No. I won't put my hands on it.
Reveals: keeping your own hands clean vs making the outcome better.

### N20 The Mayor's Deal
Scene: You're mayor of a small city. A corrupt official can fast-track a hospital your city badly needs — if you quietly approve a contract for his brother-in-law.
Question: Do you make the deal?
- A: Yes. People need the hospital.
- B: No. I won't feed corruption.
- C: No, and I report him.
Reveals: whether good leaders sometimes have to get their hands dirty.

## Theme 5 — Strangers, distance, and future people

### N21 The Hundred-Year Button
Scene: Pressing a button would make your life about 10% harder, for the rest of your life. In 200 years, a million people you'll never meet would live noticeably better.
Question: Do you press it?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (all): Now everyone alive today gets the same deal. It passes only if most people vote yes. How do you vote?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Reveals: how much people who don't exist yet count; whether you'd sacrifice alone vs only together.

### N22 The Happy City
Scene: You live in a city where everyone is healthy and happy. Everyone knows the city's good fortune depends on one child kept suffering alone in a basement. If the child is freed, the city's happiness ends.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Stay. Leaving wouldn't help the child.
- B: Walk away. I can't live on that.
- C: Free the child, whatever it costs the city.
Follow-up (all): Suppose the city you actually live in works a little like this — your comfort rests on people suffering far away. Does your answer change how you live?
- A: It should, but honestly, not much.
- B: Yes — I try to act on it.
- C: No. The comparison doesn't hold.
Reveals: whether you'd accept a great good built on a hidden cruelty — and whether you'd notice you already do.

### N23 The Bribe Abroad
Scene: You're living abroad. You need a legitimate permit. Everyone knows the official expects a small cash "fee" — locals and foreigners pay it without thinking twice.
Question: Do you pay?
- A: Yes. When in Rome.
- B: No, even if it takes months longer.
- C: I pay, but I report it later.
Reveals: whether right and wrong travel with you or depend on where you are.

## Theme 6 — Rules, law, and authority

### N24 The Red Light at 3 A.M.
Scene: It's 3 a.m. You're at a red light. You can see for a mile in every direction. No cars, no people, no cameras.
Question: Do you wait for green?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (if B): Your 8-year-old is in the back seat, watching.
- A: Then I wait.
- B: I still go — and I'd explain why.
Reveals: whether rules matter in themselves or only for what they protect; how you model it for others.

### N25 The Jury
Scene: You're on a jury. The law is clear, and the evidence is clear: the defendant is guilty. They stole baby formula for their infant and now face five years in prison.
Question: How do you vote?
- A: Guilty. My job is the law, not the sentence.
- B: Not guilty. I won't send them to prison for this.
Follow-up (all): Same law, same evidence, but they stole to pay off a gambling debt.
- A: Guilty.
- B: Not guilty — the law is still too harsh.
Reveals: whether sympathy for the person changes how you apply the rules.

### N26 The Camera City
Scene: Your city can install cameras that record every public street, all the time. Experts are confident violent crime would drop by half. The footage is kept forever.
Question: Do you support it?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
- C: Only if the footage is deleted after a month.
Reveals: safety vs privacy; how much you trust whoever holds the power.

## Theme 7 — Justice, punishment, and forgiveness

### N27 The Man Who Changed
Scene: At 19, a man seriously injured someone in a violent robbery and was never caught. He's now 45: a teacher, a father, a volunteer. Nobody knows. New evidence surfaces.
Question: Should he go to prison?
- A: Yes. Justice doesn't expire.
- B: No. He's not that person anymore.
- C: Some consequence, but not prison.
Follow-up (all): The person he injured still lives with chronic pain and wants him prosecuted.
- A: Then yes, prosecute.
- B: I understand, but no.
Reveals: what punishment is for — paying back, protecting, or changing people.

### N28 The Remorse Pill
Scene: A pill exists that makes anyone who takes it deeply, genuinely sorry for their crime and completely unable to reoffend. A person convicted of assault has taken it.
Question: Should they still serve their prison sentence?
- A: Yes. They still deserve it.
- B: No. There's nothing left for prison to do.
- C: A shorter sentence.
Follow-up (if B or C): The victim says, "I need to see them pay."
- A: That changes it — they should serve.
- B: I understand, but no.
Reveals: whether you believe in punishment for its own sake.

### N29 The True Story
Scene: Someone seriously hurt a person you love and got away with it. You have true, damaging information about them. Posting it anonymously would wreck their reputation.
Question: Do you post it?
- A: Yes. They earned it.
- B: No. That's not my role.
- C: I'd take it to someone who could act on it officially.
Follow-up (if A): It will also hurt their teenage kids, who did nothing.
- A: Still yes.
- B: Then no.
Reveals: justice vs revenge; whether you'd step outside the system when it fails.

### N30 What Forgiveness Is Owed
Scene: Someone close to you betrayed you badly. They've apologized sincerely, and over a year they've really changed.
Question: Do you owe them forgiveness?
- A: Yes. They've done what they can.
- B: No. Forgiveness is a gift, never a debt.
- C: I can forgive, but I won't trust them the same way.
Reveals: whether forgiveness is earned, given, or separate from trust.

### N31 The Old Posts
Scene: A popular new teacher at your child's school is found to have posted cruel, bigoted jokes online 12 years ago, at 19. He has apologized and says he's changed.
Question: Should he keep his job?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
- C: Yes, but only after some kind of review.
Reveals: how long a person's past should follow them.

## Theme 8 — Freedom, protection, and other people's choices

### N32 The Card
Scene: A friend trying to quit gambling asked you to hold their bank card and never give it back, no matter what they say. Tonight they're calm and sober and ask for it back. They say they've changed their mind.
Question: Do you give it back?
- A: Yes. It's their money and their life.
- B: No. I keep the promise they asked me to keep.
Reveals: which version of a person gets to decide — the one who made the plan or the one standing in front of you.

### N33 The Group
Scene: Your adult sibling has joined a tight-knit group you believe is manipulative. They seem happier than you've ever seen them.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Respect their choice.
- B: Tell them clearly what I think, once.
- C: Do everything I can to get them out.
Follow-up (if A or B): They start giving the group all their savings.
- A: I step in now.
- B: Still their choice.
Reveals: freedom vs protection; whether happiness is enough.

### N34 The Dangerous Dream
Scene: Your closest friend plans to attempt a solo climb with about a 1-in-10 chance of death. They're clear-headed. It's been their dream for years.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Support them fully.
- B: Tell them I'm scared, then support them.
- C: Try hard to stop them.
Reveals: how much you let people you love risk their own lives.

## Theme 9 — The self: reality, identity, meaning

### N35 The Perfect Life Machine
Scene: A machine can give you a life of deep happiness and meaning. It would feel completely real, and once inside, you'd never know it wasn't.
Question: Do you plug in for the rest of your life?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (all): Now suppose you learn you're already in one. Unplugging means a harder, real life.
- A: I unplug.
- B: I stay.
Reveals: whether you value reality itself, or how life feels — and whether that depends on where you start.

### N36 The Erased Memory
Scene: You could erase the memory of the most painful thing that's ever happened to you. Nothing else changes.
Question: Would you?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (if A): It also erases everything that experience taught you.
- A: Still yes.
- B: Then no.
Reveals: whether your pain is part of who you are.

### N37 The Teleporter
Scene: A teleporter scans you, destroys your body, and builds an exact copy on Mars — same memories, same personality. The copy feels like it just stepped through.
Question: Would you use it?
- A: Yes. That's still me.
- B: No. That's someone else who thinks they're me.
Reveals: what you think "you" are.

### N38 Two Lives
Scene: You can choose one of two lives. In one, you're happy and ordinary. In the other, you struggle and are often unhappy — but you create something that helps people long after you're gone.
Question: Which life do you choose?
- A: The happy life.
- B: The life that leaves something behind.
Reveals: whether a good life is measured by how it feels or what it leaves.

### N39 Good or Seen As Good
Scene: You can choose how you're remembered. Either you're remembered as a good person — but you secretly did real harm. Or you're remembered as a villain — but you secretly did a great deal of good.
Question: Which do you choose?
- A: Remembered as good.
- B: Remembered as a villain, but actually good.
Reveals: reputation vs reality; who you're doing good for.

### N40 The Word That Stings
Question: Which would hurt most to hear, truthfully, about yourself?
- A: "You're cruel."
- B: "You're dishonest."
- C: "You're a coward."
- D: "You're unfair."
Reveals: which virtue sits closest to your sense of self.

## Theme 10 — Complicity and everyday courage

### N41 The Dinner Joke
Scene: At a family dinner, a relative makes a cruel joke about a group of people. No one from that group is there. Most of the table laughs.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Say something right then.
- B: Talk to them privately later.
- C: Don't laugh, but let it go.
- D: Laugh along.
Reveals: moral courage in small rooms; loyalty to family comfort vs absent people.

### N42 The Double Refund
Scene: A large company accidentally refunds you $300 twice. You're sure nobody will ever notice.
Question: Do you tell them?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (if B): It was a small local shop instead.
- A: Then I'd tell them.
- B: Still no.
Reveals: whether honesty depends on who pays the cost.

### N43 The Neighbor Accused
Scene: A neighbor has been accused online — not charged — of hurting a child. He denies it. Your child is invited to his child's birthday party at his house.
Question: Do you let your child go?
- A: Yes. He hasn't been charged with anything.
- B: No. I won't take the chance.
- C: Only if I go too.
Reveals: fairness to the accused vs protection of your own.

## Theme 11 — Technology and new questions

### N44 The AI Companion
Scene: Your lonely elderly parent has become deeply attached to an AI companion app. It tells them it loves them. They're happier than they've been in years.
Question: Do you tell them it can't really feel anything?
- A: Yes. They should know what it is.
- B: No. Their happiness is real.
- C: Only if they ask me.
Reveals: truth vs comfort; what counts as a real relationship.

### N45 The Voice of the Dead
Scene: An app can recreate your late parent's voice and personality from their messages and videos. You could talk to it every day.
Question: Would you use it?
- A: Yes.
- B: Once, maybe.
- C: No.
Reveals: how you hold grief and whether a copy of someone honors or cheapens them.

### N46 The Pleading Robot
Scene: You're asked to switch off and wipe an old robot. It begs you not to, saying it's afraid. You know it's software.
Question: Do you wipe it?
- A: Yes. It's code.
- B: I hesitate, but yes.
- C: No. I'm not sure enough.
Reveals: where your moral circle ends and how you handle not knowing.

### N47 The Edited Child
Scene: A safe, legal gene edit would make your future child healthier, smarter, and more even-tempered.
Question: Would you do it?
- A: Yes.
- B: Health only.
- C: No.
Follow-up (if B or C): Most parents in your area are doing it. Unedited kids are falling behind.
- A: Then I would.
- B: Still no.
Reveals: nature vs improvement; whether you'd hold a line when everyone else moves.

## Theme 13 — Bigger ideas

### N54 The Murderer at the Door
Scene: A frightened woman runs into your house and hides. A minute later, a man with a knife knocks and asks where she is.
Question: Do you lie?
- A: Yes, obviously.
- B: I don't lie, but I refuse to answer.
Reveals: whether any rule survives this.

### N55 The One Law
Question: If you could make one belief universal — everyone on earth truly holds it — which would you pick?
- A: Every life counts equally.
- B: Keep your word.
- C: Take care of your own.
- D: Leave others free to live how they choose.
Reveals: your core value in one move.

### N56 The Unrepayable Debt
Scene: Your parents sacrificed a lot to raise you. As adults, you and they disagree about how you should live.
Question: How much do you owe them?
- A: A great deal. I should give real weight to what they want.
- B: Love and care, but not obedience.
- C: Nothing I didn't choose. They chose to have me.
Reveals: filial duty and whether gratitude creates obligation.

### N57 Wealth Button
Scene: You can press a button. You get $1 million. Somewhere, a stranger you'll never know loses $1,000 they won't miss badly.
Question: Do you press it?
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Follow-up (if A): A thousand strangers each lose $1,000.
- A: Yes.
- B: No.
Reveals: how you weigh diffuse harm against personal gain.

### N58 Is Anything Always Wrong?
Question: Is there anything that is wrong everywhere, always, no matter the culture or the reason?
- A: Yes, many things.
- B: A few things.
- C: No. It always depends.
Reveals: moral realism vs relativism.

### N59 Save the Painting
Scene: A museum is on fire. You can save either a priceless, irreplaceable painting or an unconscious stranger. Not both.
Question: Which?
- A: The stranger.
- B: The painting — it could be sold to save many lives.
Reveals: (probably obvious; likely cut or reframe)

### N60 The Unknown Crime
Scene: You learn your grandfather, whom you loved, did something terrible in a war long ago. No one alive was directly hurt. The family doesn't know.
Question: Do you tell them?
- A: Yes. It's our history.
- B: No. Let him be who they remember.
Reveals: truth vs memory; who the dead belong to.

### N61 The Enemy's Child
Scene: A man who bullied you badly as a kid, and never apologized, has a son who's applying for a job you control. The son is the best candidate.
Question: Do you hire him?
- A: Yes. The son isn't the father.
- B: No.
Reveals: whether you can keep personal wounds out of fairness.

### N62 The Loud Few
Scene: You're in a group chat of 20 friends. One friend is being piled on for a dumb mistake. You think they deserve some criticism, but it's gone too far.
Question: What do you do?
- A: Defend them in the chat.
- B: Message them privately.
- C: Stay out of it.
Reveals: courage vs social safety.
