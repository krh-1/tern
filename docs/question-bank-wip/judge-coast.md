## Notes for Ken

- **Follow-ups that add drama rather than a new fact.** The 2026-10-04 pass leaned on two moves: multiply the number (D2, D9, Q4: "2,000", "50 times") and make the victim face you (D2, D9). Each works once. Used three times in one world, players learn the move and stop being surprised. A good follow-up changes one thing that separates two reasons.
- **Follow-ups that are a ~90% flip.** Raising the cost on the "good" answer until nearly everyone flips (sister's wedding, hungry family) measures nothing and then feeds a portrait line almost everyone gets. A follow-up should be hard for the people it fires for, not just painful.
- **Tendency wiring is looser than the questions.** Several answers feed lines they don't support: D4 into "You step in when someone's being hurt" (no one is hurt), D4 into "You treat family and strangers the same", D12 into "You judge people by their reasons", B25 into "You're harder on yourself than on others", and "I already don't eat meat" into "You take animals' lives seriously". Each one puts a line in front of a player that a stranger reading their answers wouldn't agree with. Worth one wiring check per world.
- **"Outcomes" scored by word, not meaning.** D12 scores "reward what they produced" as Outcomes because "results" sounds like "outcomes". The axis means judging a choice by its consequences. Check other worlds for the same pun.
- **The Coast leans hard on one trade-off.** D2, Q4, D9 and B22 all ask "the few you can see, or the many you can't". That's the world's theme, but four of twelve is a lot. My D9 and Q4 rewrites make each one separate a different reason, which helps.
- **D12 is off-theme.** A manager splitting a bonus is about merit at work, not strangers, sacrifice or people far away. It reads like a City question. It can stay, but if a Coast slot opens up, this is the one to move.
- **D2's title.** "The Anonymous Donor" describes nothing in the question. `The Neighbor's Rent` says what it is about. D9's "The Sacrifice Calculus" has the same problem (proposed above: `The Two Programs`).
- **Self-sacrifice answers are cheap in a game.** "I'll go" (B15) and "Yes" to a kidney (B19) cost nothing to click. Both are kept because a follow-up presses on them, and that should be the rule for any self-sacrifice answer.
