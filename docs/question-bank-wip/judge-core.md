# Judge — Core 12 for World 1 (the park map)

> **Status:** recommendation for Ken's ruling. Not canonical. Axis reads for bank questions (B-IDs) are my inference; the bank carries no nudges yet.
>
> **The big why:** the core 12 does three jobs at once. It must (1) give a real read on all five axes even if someone answers only 4–6 of them in random order, (2) add up to a meaningful portrait after all 12, and (3) be the shared set friends compare. The proposed 12 is strong on (3) but weak on (1): it leans hard on "who do you save" and under-probes three axes.

**Terms used here**
- **Primary probe:** the question's answers push an axis hard (roughly a nudge of 2 or more, in either direction). **Secondary:** a lighter or follow-up-only push.
- **Axes:** O/R Outcomes–Rules · C/I Collective–Individual · H/T Heart–Thought · L/P Loyalty–Principle · S/D System–Disruption.
- **Heavy / medium / light:** heavy = death or grave harm; medium = real stakes, no death; light = low commitment, playful or abstract.

Notes on the reads:
- **Q1** counts for H because its mother follow-up fires for everyone and swings H by 2–3.
- **B18** is a textbook Heart–Thought split as well as a proximity one: "being right there makes it mine" is the felt pull; "a child is a child" is impartial reasoning. Proximity lives on L/P per AGENTS.md (the seven-to-five axis reduction).
- **B12 warning — the direction is ambiguous.** "Same choice, same wrong" is how both strict rule-followers *and* consequentialists (who judge by expected harm, not by luck) answer. "The one who hit the child did worse" is outcome-based judging, but it is not the same thing as outcome-based *acting*. Scoring it on O/R risks pushing people the wrong way. It is a great conversation question and a poor axis instrument.
- **B45** measures envy, which is not one of the five axes. It feeds the portrait paragraph but not the code, and "the sting" is an answer people are reluctant to give honestly, so most will pick A.

**What's missing from the proposed set that matters:**
1. **A question about living inside a system**, like a queue, a workplace or the rules everyone follows, where "working within it" is a real choice with a cost. B29 and B09 test breaking rules; nothing tests trusting them. That's why S/D is thin.
2. **Fair shares:** who deserves what, whether equality or merit. Right now "fairness" is only moral luck (B12).
3. **Choices where it costs *you*.** B21 is the only one. Most of the questions ask you to judge a situation from outside or to pick someone to save. The owner wants "who you are", and that shows most clearly when your own stake is on the line.

Still missing even after the swaps, and fine to leave for world 2: punishment and forgiveness (B33, B36), animals (B24), machines and AI (Q11, B26), moral luck (B12).

## D) Mix

- **Too heavy:** 8 of 12 proposed are heavy (Q1, B09, B02, B12, B18, Q11, B24, plus B12's child). Three involve a child being hurt or dying. The bank's own rule caps heavy scenes at two in a row, and free roam makes that impossible to enforce. With 8 heavy in a pool of 12, most random walks will string three together. **The recommended set has 4 heavy, 5 medium and 3 light**, so a random path rarely hits three heavy scenes in a row.
- **Settings:** proposed has two road crashes (B12, Q11) and two rescues from immediate death (B18 pond, B24 fire), and zero scenes at work or in public institutions. The recommended set's settings are a rail yard, a 2 a.m. phone call, a hospital bed, a thought experiment, a pond, a courtroom, a friend's kitchen, a hospital waiting list, a family dinner, an office, a button and "an invisible year". There are no duplicate settings.
- **Answer count:** nearly everything is two-option, which suits the "did you pull the lever?" comparison. B40 (3 options) and the B18 follow-up (3) add some variety. That's enough. Don't add quick-read four-option questions to the core; they're pace-changers, not iconic questions.
- **Repetitive pairs in the proposed set:**
  - **Q1 and Q11:** the same 5-vs-1 structure. Q11's policy angle adds some C, but friends will experience it as "the trolley again, in a car".
  - **B18 and B24:** both rescues, both about whose life counts. B24 also repeats Q1's mother follow-up (your person vs a stranger).
  - **Q1 follow-up, B09 and B24:** three "your own vs others" tests. That over-feeds L/P and under-feeds everything else.
  - **B02 and B09:** both about lying, but on different axes (comfort vs loyalty). This pair is fine; keep both.

## E) Recommendation — final 12

**Scoring follow-ups (not rulings, just flags):**
- The bank questions need nudges written before any of this works, and B01's S/D read depends on how its follow-up is scored.
- The Q5 conflict-avoidance pattern (AGENTS.md) disappears from the core, because Q5 isn't in it. It needs a new home or an explicit retirement.
- Free roam means no core follow-up may depend on another core question's answer. All 12 above meet that; Q1's nested follow-up depends only on Q1 itself.

### Best first stops (near the park entrance)

1. **B21 The Million-Dollar Button.** Light, a one-line setup, answered in seconds, and the follow-up flips you right away. The one-answer note writes itself ("You'd take a small harm to a stranger for a big gain. Let's see where your line is."). Low commitment and instantly gripping.
2. **Q1 The Trolley.** Everyone recognizes it, so it's the social hook ("did you pull the lever?"), and the mother follow-up is the fastest way to show that Tern goes deeper than a meme.

Put B02 and B09 deeper in the park, so a new user's first three stops can't all be heavy. B01 is a good third stop near the entrance.

### What a user should see after all 12

1. **The code reveal:** five letters, one at a time, as specified in AGENTS.md.
2. **The full portrait:** the one-line summary, then the radar chart, the five axis rows and the paragraph (the fixed distinction-report order).
3. **"What you protect / what you'll trade"**, now final rather than provisional.
4. **"Where you're torn":** the one or two sharpest tensions, named with the actual questions behind them (for example, "You told your grandfather the truth, but covered for your sibling").
5. **A comparison hook:** "Send your 12 to a friend and see where you split." This is the social payoff of a fixed core. *Ken's call:* whether to show "how most people answered". It isn't a progress number, but it is a number.
6. **World 2 unlock**, with a teaser pointing at the user's lowest-confidence axis ("There's something about how you treat rules we want to explore.").
