# Neighborhood: questions for Ken to rule on

**Ken's ruling (2026-10-03): keep the questions and scoring as they are; none of these were applied.**

Nothing here has been applied. The question and answer wording in `game/content-neighborhood.js` is word-for-word from the docs (dashes became periods, as in the park). Each item below gives my recommendation.

## Wording and follow-ups

1. **B10 (What We Owe Our Parents) has no follow-up.** It's one of the heaviest questions, and a single answer can't show *why* you chose it. Two people who say "No" may be thinking of a debt they owe, or just of how much they'd miss their parents.
   **Recommendation:** add one for both answers, such as: "Your parents then say they'll accept the marriage if you move back to their town." Or, for "Yes": "Ten years later, your parents are ill and ask to reconcile." Have the judges check it first.

2. **B55 (The Bent Rule) has no follow-up.** It's light, so that's acceptable. But on its own, "Yes, and they give the win back" can feel like the parenting answer people are expected to give, so it may be close to the 80% line.
   **Recommendation:** add a follow-up for "Yes": "Their friends would never know, and giving it back would embarrass your child in front of them." That puts a real cost on the expected answer.

3. **B45's follow-up shows very little** ("You learn they got it mostly through luck… easier / harder"). Neither answer clearly leans one way on any value, so I gave it only a light pull on group vs. self (CI ±1). The main question is also about a feeling, not a choice.
   **Recommendation:** keep it for now, since it's light and people like answering it. Later, swap in a follow-up where you have to act, such as: "They ask you to give a toast at their celebration."

4. **Q5, "Honor my parent's request. Take it all."** The will is legally binding and splits the estate equally, so the answer doesn't say how you'd "take it all". (Hide the request? Ask your sibling to give up their share?) Players will each picture something different.
   **Recommendation:** change the answer to "Honor my parent's request. Ask my sibling to give up their share." Or add a setup line: "Your sibling would sign whatever you put in front of them." This changes how the answer is read, so it's your call.

5. **Q3: both follow-ups ask "will you lie to one friend for another?"** That's fine. The open question is FU-B, "I say I don't know". It's a direct lie to a friend's face, so it may deserve weight 2, like the park's highest-stakes follow-ups. I left it at 1.5.

## Escape answers

6. **B42, "Keep my distance."** This is a quiet middle: you don't accuse your friend and you don't stand by them. It has a real cost (your friend loses you), so I think it survives the rule. **Recommendation:** keep it.

7. **B43, "I let it go"** (after your neighbor spreads the story that you admitted you were wrong). It's a passive answer, but your reputation pays the price, so it isn't free. **Recommendation:** keep it.

## Scoring calls I made that differ from the old docs

8. **D7 (the fiancé), sign flipped on Loyalty vs Principle.** The old doc scored "Tell them" as Principle and "Stay quiet" as Loyalty. With today's axis meanings, telling your close friend is siding with someone close to you over a near-stranger (the fiancé), which is Loyalty. Staying quiet out of fairness to the fiancé is Principle. I scored it that way, at a light ±1.

9. **D1 and Q5: Loyalty taken out.** The old doc scored them as Loyalty, but both choices are between people close to you (your parent and your family, or your parent and your sibling), not between your own people and strangers. I scored them on promise vs. outcome (D1), working within the rules vs. breaking them (Q5), and heart vs. head instead.

10. **System vs Disruption is thin in this world** (only B55, B10 and Q5 measure it). The Neighborhood is about people, not institutions. B10's System score treats your parents' ultimatum as an authority you accept or refuse. That's a judgment call. **Recommendation:** accept it. The City will carry most of System vs Disruption.
